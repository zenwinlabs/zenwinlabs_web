The science

Your brain is not broken. It was hijacked.

Nicotine floods your dopamine system and rewires it over years. When you quit, your brain isn't craving a cigarette — it's demanding a chemical it learned to depend on. That demand peaks at 3–5 minutes and fades. Every time. ClearBreath makes that biology visible, survivable, and over time — irrelevant.

The craving window

A craving cannot survive 5 minutes of this.

Cravings live in your visuospatial working memory — the part of your brain that builds mental imagery. Games that occupy that same cognitive space don't just distract you. They make it physically impossible for the craving to fully form. Five games, each calibrated to outlast a craving peak.

  • Focus tap — occupies the hand-to-mouth association that fuels habitual smoking

On slipping

One cigarette is not a relapse.

The Abstinence Violation Effect — the cognitive distortion that turns one slip into a full relapse — is responsible for more failed quits than any other single factor. It is not weakness. It is a well-understood psychological pattern.

When you log a slip in ClearBreath, a post-slip recovery protocol activates two hours later. Not immediately, not judgmentally — two hours later, when you are calm enough to hear it. It walks you through three specific cognitive reframes that dismantle the all-or-nothing thinking before it can take hold.

Because the goal is not a perfect record. The goal is a permanent quit.
